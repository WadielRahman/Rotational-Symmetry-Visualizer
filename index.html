🧊 3D Symmetry

Lines & axes of symmetry

Spin it. Does it look the same?

1 · Definition

2D line of symmetry: fold the shape along the line and the two halves match exactly.
3D axis of symmetry: a line through a solid. Turn the solid about it by less than 360° and it looks unchanged.

2 · Everyday analogy

A paper butterfly folds in half (a line). A plain round bottle looks the same as you spin it on a table (an axis).

A square has 4 lines of symmetry (2D). A solid has axes (3D).

4 · Worked example

A cylinder has a main axis through the centres of its two circular faces. Turn it by any angle about this line and it looks identical.

Quick check: how many lines of symmetry does a rectangle (not a square) have?

2 (one horizontal, one vertical). The diagonals are not lines of symmetry.

Order of rotational symmetry

How many times does it fit onto itself in one full turn?

1 · Definition

The order of rotational symmetry is the number of times a solid fits exactly onto itself during one complete 360° turn about a specified axis.

Order = 360° ÷ smallest angle of rotation

2 · Everyday analogy

A square coffee coaster: turn it a quarter-turn and it still covers the same spot. 4 fits per full turn.

Always name the axis. A cube has order 4 about a face-to-face axis, order 3 about a vertex-to-vertex axis and order 2 about an edge-to-edge axis.

4 · Worked examples

Cube, through centres of opposite faces90° → 4
Square-based prism, principal axis90° → 4
Equilateral triangular prism, principal axis120° → 3
Cylinder, central axis∞
Sphere, any axis through centre∞
Quick check: order of a cube about an axis through opposite vertices?

3 (smallest turn 120°).

Planes of symmetry

Cut it in two. Are the halves mirror images?

1 · Definition

A plane of symmetry cuts a solid into two identical halves that are mirror images of each other.

2 · Everyday analogy

Stand in front of a mirror: the mirror is a plane, and your reflection is the matching half.

4 · Worked examples

Cube9 (3 parallel to faces + 6 diagonal)
Cuboid (unequal sides)3
Square-based pyramid4
Regular tetrahedron6
Cylinder, sphereinfinitely many
Planes of symmetry (reflection) are not the same as order of rotational symmetry (turning). A cube has 9 planes but order 4, 3 or 2 depending on the axis.
Quick check: do the diagonal planes of a 3 × 2 × 1 cuboid give identical halves?

No. Only the 3 planes parallel to the faces do, so a cuboid has 3 planes.

Dark and light halves show the mirror pair.
